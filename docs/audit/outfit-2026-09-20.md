# Outfit audit, 20 September 2026 — preview blazer, delivered light blue shirt

**Report.** One order showed a navy blazer in the free preview and a light blue shirt in
all four delivered photographs, "although the prompt text is identical".

**Finding.** The prompt text is *not* identical. Everything else is. The model obeyed
both times.

---

It would not have settled the question if it were. `app/adapters/fal.py:106-112` logs

and `grep -rn "prompt" app/*.py app/adapters/*.py | grep logger` returns nothing. The

follow-up below rather than fixed here.

Photos 2, 3 and 4 differ from photo 1 in one more place, by design: the framing clause
cycles through `build_prompt`'s four variants ("subject slightly left of centre",
"three-quarter turn", "tight head-and-shoulders"). Nothing in that list is clothing.

---

**DIFFERENCE: one clause of the prompt — the preview asked for "a dark navy blazer over
a plain white shirt" and all four delivered photos asked for "a smart-casual light blue
shirt with an open collar", because `Preview.__call__` always builds its prompt with
`wardrobe=None` (app/preview.py:60) while the pipeline builds its four with
`wardrobe=order.wardrobe` (app/core.py:302), the garment the customer picked after the
preview had already been generated; every other field — model id, the single reference
photo and its order, aspect ratio 4:5, output format, absent seed, one image per call —
is byte-identical, and the only other difference is the deliberate resolution step 0.5K
→ 1K.**

## 3. Why, and why no code was changed

The selector is rendered inside `{handle ? (…)}` in `upload-form.tsx`: it does not exist
until the preview has come back. The order is upload → preview → choose clothes → pay,
so a customer who picks anything other than the default is shown one garment and sold
another. That is structural and it is ours, not the model's.

It is also already a settled, shipped decision (unit F6, `tests/test_preview_matches_delivery.py`):
the four final prompts must honour the customer's choice — that is the product they
bought — and the page must say so plainly when the choice differs from the preview. The
disclosure is live in the deployed bundle:

```

$ … | grep -o 'style:"corporativo"[^}]\{0,60\}'

```

and `test_with_the_selector_untouched_the_preview_prompt_is_the_delivered_prompt` pins
the other half: a customer who touches nothing gets the blazer in the preview and the
blazer in all four photos.

So the difference is in our code, is intended, and is disclosed. **No behaviour was
changed by this audit.** What it added is the instrument: the funnel is now walked end
to end and the fal request bodies are captured and compared, so the next report of this
kind is answered from evidence rather than from the template.

## 4. Follow-ups

- **The fal log line cannot answer this question.** It names `application`, `resolution`,
  `sources` and `latency_ms`, never the wardrobe. One `wardrobe=` key on the delivery
  call and on `checkout session created` would have made section 1 sufficient on its own.
  Not done here: it changes production logging and this task was an audit.
- **`style` and `wardrobe` are not bound to the signed preview handle.** `preview_token`
  covers `batch` and `n` only, so `/api/checkout` accepts whatever style the client sends
  after a preview that was always built with `corporativo`. Today's frontend hard-codes
  `corporativo`, so it cannot fire; a stale cached bundle could.
- **needs Kevin: read the reported order's document** — `orders/<session id>` in
  Firestore, fields `style` and `wardrobe`. Reading production was blocked for this
  session. The command, read-only:
  `gcloud firestore databases list` then the Firestore REST read of
  `projects/studio-face-fresh-start/databases/(default)/documents/orders/<session id>`.
  Expected: `style=corporativo`, `wardrobe=camisa-azul` or `blusa-clara`. If instead
  `style=linkedin`, the backdrop in the delivered photos would be a blurred office rather
  than the grey studio, and the cause is the unbound `style` above, not the picker.
